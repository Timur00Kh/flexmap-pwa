# Stretching mode: method and sources

Stretching mode contains 18 self-observations: 14 new sport-oriented movements and four existing checks (shoulder flexion, standing knee bend, knee-to-wall ankle movement, and wrist extension). Basic mode retains the original 18. The shared checks keep their existing IDs, so an observation can be shown in both modes. The combined catalogue contains each ID once; switching modes does not migrate or delete history.

Each new movement has five app-authored, ordered landmarks for self-report, not norms, clinical thresholds, or a validated test battery. Estimates of angles are approximate and do not diagnose muscle length. The landmarks are a guide to comparing the same person over time, not to comparing people: body proportions, equipment, technique, and comfort affect the result. Repeat with the same stance, foot position, support height, and setup specified in each check. A landmark is only applicable when the position can be reached under control with the stated alignment; stop for pain and never force a position to reach a level. Participants may use a lower level or skip a movement.

## Sources and scope

- [NSCA: Assessment protocols, sit and reach](https://www.nsca.com/certification/tsac-f/essentials-of-tactical-strength-training-and-conditioning-2nd-edition/excerpts/assesment-protocols-for-military-personnel-firefighters-and-police-officers/) describes a standardized box-based sit-and-reach with knees extended and a slow, controlled reach. Our seated-pike landmarks are a simple uninstrumented adaptation, not this protocol or its scoring table.
- [British Gymnastics: flexibility training safeguarding policy](https://clubhub-resources.british-gymnastics.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Safeguarding-Policy-1.pdf) describes controlled, self-directed stretching and safe practice. The app uses self-directed checks, not partner-assisted or ballistic methods; the policy is guidance for gymnastics settings and does not validate the app's scales.

The five steps are descriptive observations, not treatment advice, a diagnosis, or a promise that every person should reach the last step. The `angles` field supports the existing illustrative pose renderer and is not a measurement except where the active straight-leg raise explicitly asks for an approximate angle.
